# Bible Reference Audit Report

## Scope and result

Reviewed all **1,426 Q-numbered entries** in the 12 canonical study decks listed in `decks/manifest.json`. This includes 1,420 complete question cards and six placeholder records (Section 12 Q35–Q40) whose question content and transcripts are missing. For each question, the review considered its wording, correct answer, explanation, `REF:` timestamp, corresponding available transcript passage, and any existing `BIBLE:` field. Existing references were checked for direct relevance, not just whether the passage happened to be mentioned nearby.

The review retained **791** `BIBLE:` fields and left **635** entries without one: 629 complete questions plus the six transcript-dependent placeholders. The fields currently comprise 322 marked `Confirmed in lesson and deck`, 210 marked `Also cited in lesson segment`, and 259 marked `Added from online research`. Each researched field includes at least one source URL.

Compared with the PR branch’s starting point, the review added 5 fields to previously blank questions, removed 178 fields whose references did not directly support the specific answer, and revised 216 existing fields. Question IDs, question and answer wording, explanations, and `REF:` fields are unchanged.

## Review method

- Parsed only the canonical files in `decks/manifest.json`; generated copies are rebuilt from these sources.
- Read each question’s text, correct answer, explanation, `REF:` field, and the transcript passage identified by the lesson/timestamp when available.
- Checked whether each existing or proposed passage supports the answer in context. A nearby citation, a passage that supports only a premise, or a broad theological/historical analogy was not treated as direct evidence.
- Preferred passages cited in the lesson. For references added through research, verified the passage text and included a link to the text source in the deck field. Research sources used include ESV.org, Bible Gateway’s ESV text, Bible.com, and Biblia.com.
- Left the field blank where the answer was historical, lexical, practical, or depended on a contested interpretation that a direct passage could not establish. The unresolved table below records every such question ID and rationale.

### Field labels

- `Confirmed in lesson and deck:` — passage is named in the lesson and deck and directly supports the answer.
- `Also cited in lesson segment:` — passage is cited in the corresponding transcript segment and was retained only where the passage directly supports the answer.
- `Added from online research:` — passage was researched outside the repository; the field includes its text-source URL.

## Interpretive, disputed, and historical claims flagged

- **Predestination and foreknowledge (Section 03, Q2, Q8, Q43):** 1 Samuel 23 records a conditional warning about what would happen if David stayed in Keilah. It does not itself define the distinction between divine foreknowledge and predestination; the lesson’s distinction remains an interpretation. The deck fields preserve this qualification.
- **Oneness and the Trinity (Section 03, Q57; Section 04, Q53–Q54):** Deuteronomy 6:4 and the New Testament passages support parts of the lesson’s case, but do not state the later “one what/three whos” or “one person/two natures” formulas verbatim. These are theological syntheses; the cited verses should not be read as individually proving the full formulations.
- **Jesus and Michael (Section 04, Q16; Section 09, Q56):** Hebrews 1 contrasts the Son with angels, and John 1:3 attributes creation to the Word. Those passages support the lesson’s case against treating Jesus as a created angel, but do not name Michael as Jesus. Section 09 Q56 asks what Jehovah’s Witnesses teach; its denominational description remains uncited as a Bible claim.
- **Holy Spirit as a person (Section 09, Q133):** John 14:16–17 and 14:26 describe the Spirit as “another Helper” and use personal language. This supports the lesson’s argument for personhood; the conclusion is a theological inference from the passage, not a claim that one pronoun alone settles every aspect of the doctrine.
- **Inherited sin and infants (Section 01, Q33, Q35, Q44):** Romans 5 is relevant to the lesson’s original-sin discussion but does not settle the fate of infants or people unable to understand the gospel. The incidental fields were removed.
- **Cornelius and angelic activity (Section 02, Q84/Q92; Section 12, Q5/Q34):** Acts 10 records Cornelius’s prayers, alms, and the angel’s message. It does not establish the broader rule that a lifestyle attracts angels or prove every causal claim made in the lesson; unsupported generalizations remain uncited.
- **Baptism and church history (Section 12, including Q49, Q58, Q76, Q88, Q92, Q100):** Several answers concern historical developments, denominational beliefs, Greek word usage, or arguments from silence. Biblical passages can document specific narratives or terms but cannot independently verify the historical facts or settle disputed baptismal conclusions.
- **Spiritual-warfare claims (Section 11, including Q1, Q22, Q33):** Narrative examples and courtroom analogies do not by themselves establish general rules about demons, believer authority, or spiritual legality. Fields that offered only an analogy or incidental passage were removed.
