DECK: Discipleship Experience (Section 1) Soteriology

Q1. Why does the lesson use the example of the criminal on the cross?
A. To show that everyone receives equal earthly rewards.
B. To teach that good deeds after death determine salvation
C. To demonstrate that repentance always removes earthly consequences.
D. To illustrate that justification is a legal declaration made on the basis of Christ's substitution.
ANSWER: D

Q2. According to the lesson, what is the strongest biblical basis for concluding that condemnation comes after a person knowingly rejects the gospel?
A. Genesis 3: 11
B. John 3:18–19
C. Romans 7: 1
D. Psalm 51: 2
ANSWER: B

Q3. Which argument involving Jesus does Michael Heiser use to challenge the view that every person inherits Adam's personal guilt?
A. Jesus obeyed the Law perfectly.
B. If everyone born through Adam is automatically guilty, Jesus would also have been guilty.
C. Jesus' miracles prove He was sinless.
D. Jesus never discussed original sin.
ANSWER: B

Q4. In the salvation lesson, why are both Nicodemus and Cornelius discussed?
A. To demonstrate that both moral and religious people still needed salvation through faith in Christ.
B. To compare Jewish and Roman cultures.
C. To explain the history of evangelism.
D. To illustrate different spiritual gifts.
ANSWER: A

Q5. Which statement best summarizes the lesson's definition of being born again?
A. Becoming more disciplined in religious practice.
B. Joining a local church.
C. Living a morally upright life.
D. Receiving the life of God through faith in Christ.
ANSWER: D

Q6. According to the lesson on righteousness, believers are described as "accepted in the Beloved" because:
A. They consistently obey God's commands.
B. They no longer require repentance.
C. Christ's work secures their acceptance before God.
D. They possess greater spiritual gifts than others.
ANSWER: C

Q7. Which statement best reflects the lesson's explanation of right standing with God?
A. It depends on maintaining perfect obedience.
B. It depends on sincere intentions.
C. It is grounded in faith in Christ rather than personal behaviour.
D. It is earned through consistent repentance.
ANSWER: C

Q8. Why does the lesson distinguish between metamelomai and metanoia?
A. To argue that sorrow alone is insufficient because it should lead to repentance.
B. To show they can be used similarly.
C. To teach that repentance belongs only to the Old Testament.
D. To show the role of emotions in repentance.
ANSWER: A

Q9. What role does the pornography illustration serve in the lesson?
A. To explain discipline and it's role in a believer.
B. To show that behavioural change alone is not the same as gospel repentance.
C. To teach that addiction can be overcome just by making changes.
D. To argue against moral discipline.
ANSWER: B

Q10. Why does the lesson distinguish redemption from justification?
A. Redemption describes Christ's saving work, while justification describes God's legal declaration concerning the believer.
B. Redemption applies to Old Testament, while justification applies to the Church now.
C. Redemption concerns forgiveness, while justification concerns rewards.
D. They are presented as completely unrelated doctrines.
ANSWER: A

Q11. Apostle's final position on original sin is that humanity inherits:
A. Adam's guilt alone.
B. Mortality together with an irresistible tendency to sin.
C. Physical death without any inclination toward sin.
D. Immediate spiritual condemnation at birth.
ANSWER: B

Q12. Which statement best reflects the lesson's teaching about the moment a person is saved?
A. At water baptism.
B. At the altar call.
C. At the point of genuine faith in Christ.
D. After completing discipleship classes.
ANSWER: C

Q13. According to the lesson, why does Paul repeatedly affirm believers' identity before correcting their conduct?
A. Because identity provides the foundation from which lasting transformation occurs.
B. Because behaviour is unimportant.
C. Because discipline belongs only to church leaders.
D. Because sin disappears after conversion.
ANSWER: A

Q14. What is the significance of Luke 24:47 in the repentance lesson?
A. It limits repentance to the nation of Israel.
B. It shows repentance and forgiveness are to be proclaimed to all nations.
C. It teaches repentance is unnecessary after Christ's resurrection.
D. It defines repentance as sorrow alone.
ANSWER: B

Q15. The lesson explains that once God declares a believer justified:
A. Other accusations remain equally authoritative.
B. The believer must continually earn that verdict.
C. No other opinion overrides God's verdict.
D. The believer no longer faces any earthly consequences.
ANSWER: C

Q16. Which statement best integrates the teaching across the salvation and righteousness lessons?
A. Spiritual life begins through faith in Christ, and that new identity becomes the basis for transformed living.
B. Spiritual growth must precede salvation.
C. Good behaviour produces spiritual life.
D. Identity in Christ replaces the need for obedience.
ANSWER: A

Q17. Across the week's lessons, which recurring pattern best describes how God transforms people?
A. Behaviour changes first, then God grants acceptance.
B. God grants acceptance in Christ, and that reality shapes a transformed life.
C. Fear of judgment produces lasting holiness.
D. Religious discipline creates spiritual life.
ANSWER: B

Q18. Which statement best captures the relationship between faith and repentance as presented throughout the lessons?
A. Faith removes any expectation of changed living.
B. Repentance earns salvation before faith.
C. Faith in Christ brings salvation, while genuine repentance accompanies that faith and produces its fruit.
D. Repentance and faith are unrelated ideas.
ANSWER: C

Q19. Looking across all five lessons, which statement most accurately summarizes the central message?
A. Christianity is primarily about becoming morally disciplined.
B. Salvation is God's gracious work in Christ, received through faith, resulting in a new identity that produces transformed living.
C. The Christian life is sustained mainly through religious activities.
D. The gospel is chiefly a call to improve personal behaviour.
ANSWER: B

Q20. Which statement best explains why the lesson begins by discussing God's role as Judge before defining justification?
A. To establish that justification should be understood as a legal declaration rather than merely a feeling of forgiveness.
B. To show that believers should fear God's judgment every day.
C. To demonstrate that Old Testament believers understood salvation exactly as Christians do today.
D. To explain why every Christian will receive earthly rewards.
ANSWER: A

Q21. In the lesson on original sin, what distinction is presented as central to interpreting Romans 5:12?
A. Humanity inherited Adam's personal guilt.
B. Humanity inherited death and an eventual tendency to sin rather than Adam's personal guilt.
C. Humanity inherited neither death nor sin from Adam.
D. Humanity only inherited physical weakness from Adam.
ANSWER: B

Q22. The CNDP framework introduced in the salvation lesson is primarily intended to help believers:
A. Memorize the order of the New Testament books.
B. Explain salvation in a complete and coherent way.
C. Compare different theological traditions.
D. Study biblical Greek more effectively.
ANSWER: B

Q23. Paul's approach to correcting the Corinthians' moral failures was to:
A. First question whether they were truly saved.
B. Focus primarily on punishment as motivation for holiness.
C. Emphasize their identity in Christ before addressing their conduct.
D. Remove members involved in every form of sin before teaching them.
ANSWER: C

Q24. Which statement best captures the lesson's understanding of genuine repentance?
A. It is turning to Christ in faith, producing an inner change that naturally bears fruit in conduct.
B. It is behavioural reform that earns God's acceptance.
C. It is simply feeling remorse for past actions.
D. It is abandoning sinful habits before believing the gospel.
ANSWER: A

Q25. The courtroom illustration used in the justification lesson primarily emphasizes that:
A. Every believer will eventually stand trial again.
B. Justification changes a person's legal standing before God because of Christ's substitution.
C. Christians must defend themselves before God.
D. Good works overturn past guilt.
ANSWER: B

Q26. Why does Apostle disagree, at least in part, with Michael Heiser's final conclusion?
A. He believes Adam's guilt alone explains human sinfulness.
B. He believes children should be considered guilty from birth.
C. He believes Romans 5 should not be used when discussing original sin.
D. He believes mortality alone cannot explain why every human eventually sins.
ANSWER: D

Q27. Which illustration is used to explain that a person may appear physically alive while lacking spiritual life?
A. A courtroom verdict
B. A ledger balancing debts
C. A funeral where the body looked unchanged
D. A child learning to walk
ANSWER: C

Q28. According to the lesson on righteousness, why does Paul remind believers that their bodies are temples of the Holy Spirit?
A. To convince them they have permanently lost God's presence.
B. To use their identity in Christ as the basis for transformed behaviour.
C. To teach that holiness depends on human effort.
D. To discourage participation in public worship.
ANSWER: B

Q29. Why does the lesson distinguish between repentance and its fruit?
A. Because repentance is an inward turning to God that should eventually produce outward obedience.
B. Because behavioural change has no place in the Christian life.
C. Because repentance only applies before salvation.
D. Because obedience and repentance always mean the same thing.
ANSWER: A

Q30. The Greek word dikaio, as explained in the lesson, most accurately means to:
A. Become morally perfect over time.
B. Be declared righteous or acquitted.
C. Earn righteousness through obedience.
D. Receive power for holy living.
ANSWER: B
