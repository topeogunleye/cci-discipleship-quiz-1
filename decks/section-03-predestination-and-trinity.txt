DECK: Discipleship Experience (Section 3) Predestination and Trinity

Q1. Why does Apostle point to "Spirit of Christ," "Spirit of God," and "Spirit of His Son"?
A. To show that the Spirit carries different identities in different ages.
B. To demonstrate that Scripture speaks of the same Spirit in relation to the Father and the Son.
C. To distinguish the ministry of Jesus from that of the Spirit.
D. To prove Paul preferred figurative language.
ANSWER: B

Q2. If a believer says, "I lost my job because God specifically willed it." What will be the most consistent response according to the lesson?
A. Every painful event is individually ordained by God.
B. God may have known it would happen, but not every painful event is His predetermined will.
C. Painful events only happen because believers lack faith.
D. Since God is sovereign, human choices have no real influence on life's events.
ANSWER: B

Q3. Why is the word Trinity compared with words like rapture and omniscient?
A. To show that theology often creates words to describe biblical truths.
B. To show that every theological term came from church councils.
C. To prove that every theological word appears in Scripture.
D. To argue that Christians should avoid theological language.
ANSWER: A

Q4. What is the primary danger of reading Romans 8:28–30 without continuing into Romans 9–11?
A. One may misunderstand Paul's teaching on spiritual gifts.
B. One may confuse justification with glorification.
C. One may overlook the broader discussion concerning Jews and Gentiles.
D. One may ignore the historical setting of the Roman church.
ANSWER: C

Q5. What was the role of the Council of Nicaea regarding the doctrine of the Trinity?
A. It created a doctrine previously unknown.
B. It introduced new Scriptures into the canon.
C. It pointed to biblical evidence that already existed.
D. It replaced Old Testament teaching with New Testament theology.
ANSWER: C

Q6. Why is the illustration of a dog on a leash used?
A. Human beings possess unrestricted freedom.
B. God frequently overrides every human decision.
C. God's sovereignty removes human responsibility.
D. Human freedom operates within limits of God's ultimate purposes.
ANSWER: D

Q7. What observation from Genesis 18 does Apostle emphasize before discussing Abraham's intercession?
A. Abraham immediately recognized all three visitors as angels.
B. One of the three visitors is identified in the narrative as the Lord.
C. Abraham misunderstood the identity of the visitors.
D. The visitors never spoke directly to Abraham.
ANSWER: B

Q8. Which statement best distinguishes foreknowledge from predestination?
A. Everything God foreknows must occur because He predetermined it.
B. Foreknowledge and predestination describe the same aspect of God's sovereignty.
C. God may know an event will occur without ordaining or causing it to happen.
D. Predestination exists because human beings do not possess genuine free will.
ANSWER: C

Q9. Why does the lesson focus on the Hebrew word echad in Deuteronomy 6:4?
A. It always refers to a single individual.
B. It is only used when speaking about God.
C. It means exactly the same as yahid.
D. It can describe a unified plurality rather than absolute singularity.
ANSWER: D

Q10. What gives believers confidence even when others exercise free will against them?
A. God prevents every harmful decision before it happens.
B. Human beings will eventually choose what is right.
C. God's ultimate purpose cannot be frustrated by the free choices of people.
D. Every difficult circumstance was specifically designed by God.
ANSWER: C

Q11. Which conclusion best reflects the relationship between Romans 8:28 and free will?
A. God works through circumstances He did not necessarily ordain to accomplish His ultimate purpose for those who love Him.
B. Since all things work together for good, every event must have been individually predetermined by God.
C. Human free will only exists until God decides otherwise.
D. God's sovereignty eliminates the significance of human choices.
ANSWER: A

Q12. In Ephesians 1:11–13, who are "we who first trusted in Christ"?
A. All believers everywhere.
B. The apostles alone.
C. The Jews.
D. The Gentile believers.
ANSWER: C

Q13. Which part of the Trinity's definition must not be omitted?
A. That the three persons perform different ministries.
B. That the three persons are co-equal and co-eternal.
C. That the Son appears more frequently in Scripture.
D. That the Father alone possesses supreme authority.
ANSWER: B

Q14. What attitude should accompany discovering difficult truths about God's nature?
A. Suspend belief until every question is answered.
B. Accept only what appears logically consistent.
C. Receive what Scripture teaches with humility.
D. Avoid doctrines that seem mysterious.
ANSWER: C

Q15. Why was God able to declare that the people of Keilah would surrender David?
A. His perfect knowledge of human nature made Him know how they would respond.
B. Their betrayal had already become unavoidable.
C. God intended to force them into betraying David.
D. David's departure confirmed God's decree.
ANSWER: A

Q16. What conclusion can we draw from Acts 26:5 regarding the word "foreknew"?
A. It always refers to God's eternal knowledge of future events.
B. It is a technical theological term used only by Paul.
C. Its meaning depends on context and is not inherently a reference to divine omniscience.
D. It always describes God's covenant relationship with Israel.
ANSWER: C

Q17. Which two truths must both be upheld for a correct understanding of the Trinity?
A. Unity and authority.
B. Distinction and equality.
C. Glory and power.
D. Love and holiness.
ANSWER: B

Q18. What principle is reflected by the movement between Genesis, Isaiah, Psalms, Daniel, John, Hebrews, Acts, and Revelation?
A. Difficult doctrines should rest on the cumulative witness of Scripture.
B. Every doctrine should begin in the New Testament.
C. Old Testament passages are only illustrations.
D. Individual verses should be interpreted independently.
ANSWER: A

Q19. What interpretive mistake leads many readers to misunderstand predestination in Ephesians 1?
A. Reading poetry as literal doctrine.
B. Ignoring the original language.
C. Confusing salvation with inheritance.
D. Reading a corporate discussion as though it were about individuals.
ANSWER: D

Q20. "Whatever you are looking for, you will find...There is a scripture for everything you believe." What principle is being warned against?
A. Depending too much on Bible translations.
B. Ignoring the original biblical languages.
C. Looking for verses that support ideas you already hold.
D. Relying too heavily on cross-references.
ANSWER: C

Q21. Someone says the Trinity can't be biblical since the word isn't in Scripture. What is your best response?
A. Every biblical doctrine must appear by name in Scripture.
B. A doctrine is biblical if Scripture teaches the concept, even if the term is not used.
C. The Trinity became biblical after church councils accepted it.
D. The Trinity is mainly based on Christian tradition.
ANSWER: B

Q22. Why does Apostle repeatedly appeal to Old Testament passages when discussing the Trinity?
A. To show that later Christian writers misunderstood them.
B. To demonstrate that the doctrine was already present before the New Testament.
C. To establish that the New Testament should be interpreted through Judaism alone.
D. To prove that every Old Testament appearance of God refers only to Christ.
ANSWER: B

Q23. Which observation from Ephesians 1 forms the foundation of the interpretation of predestination?
A. The inheritance belongs only to the apostles.
B. God's will excludes every human response.
C. Paul's repeated change in audience throughout the passage must be accounted for.
D. Every occurrence of "predestined" always refers to salvation.
ANSWER: C

Q24. According to the lesson, what should be the primary test of any doctrine about God?
A. Whether it agrees with human reasoning.
B. Whether respected scholars accept it.
C. Whether it can be illustrated from nature.
D. Whether it is taught by Scripture.
ANSWER: D

Q25. Why does Apostle repeatedly distinguish between "we" and "you" in Ephesians 1?
A. To distinguish the apostles from ordinary believers.
B. To show that Paul's argument distinguishes Jewish believers from Gentile believers.
C. To identify mature Christians and immature Christians.
D. To explain the difference between believers before and after Pentecost.
ANSWER: B

Q26. Why is it important for Christians to study doctrines such as the Trinity?
A. To become more effective in theological debates.
B. To answer sincere questions about their faith with biblical understanding.
C. To master historical church controversies.
D. To avoid reading non-Christian literature.
ANSWER: B

Q27. Why is the parable in Matthew 20 and Matthew 22 used when discussing "called"?
A. To prove that every use of the word has exactly the same meaning.
B. To establish that "called" always refers to believers.
C. To demonstrate that the Greek word has only one possible definition.
D. To let Jesus' own use of the term shape how Paul's language is understood.
ANSWER: D

Q28. How does Genesis 2:24 support the explanation of echad?
A. It uses the same word to describe two persons united as one.
B. It teaches that marriage represents the Trinity.
C. It shows that Adam and Eve became one person.
D. It replaces the meaning of Deuteronomy 6:4.
ANSWER: A

Q29. Apostle challenges a student's interpretation of Romans 8:29 by opening other passages, what principle is he modeling?
A. Difficult passages should only be interpreted using the original Greek.
B. Every doctrine should be established from multiple Bible translations.
C. Individual verses should be interpreted within the flow of the Bible's larger argument.
D. New Testament doctrines should always be explained from the Old Testament first.
ANSWER: C

Q30. Which statement best summarizes the overall philosophy of biblical interpretation in this lesson?
A. Strong theological systems should determine how individual passages are read.
B. Greek word studies are sufficient to settle difficult doctrines.
C. Logical deductions should take priority whenever Scripture appears unclear.
D. Scripture must interpret Scripture, with context governing the meaning of theological terms.
ANSWER: D
