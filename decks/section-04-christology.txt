DECK: Discipleship Experience (Section 4) Christology

Q1. In explaining Luke 24:25–27, how does the lesson define the theological term "hermeneutical framework"?
A. A systematic method of memorizing Hebrew root words to translate the Old Testament accurately.
B. A practical, step-by-step strategy for conducting effective soul-winning in urban centers.
C. A specific lens or science of Bible interpretation used to read and understand the scriptures.
D. An ancient church creed established to guard local congregations against heresies.
ANSWER: C

Q2. Why did Jesus address the two sad disciples on the road to Emmaus as "foolish ones and slow of heart" instead of offering immediate emotional comfort?
A. Because they had abandoned the apostles in Jerusalem during a time of intense political persecution.
B. Because the realm of the spirit operates on the consciousness of the Word, not on emotions
C. Because they refused to believe the eyewitness testimony of the women who had visited the empty tomb that morning.
D. Because their grief was causing them to walk away from their assigned geographic ministry destination.
ANSWER: B

Q3. What detail does the lesson highlight to prove that John 1:1 was written as a deliberate theological commentary on Genesis 1:1?
A. Both books were originally transcribed in Aramaic during the exilic period in Babylon.
B. The Greek Septuagint translation of Genesis 1:1 and John 1:1 open with the exact same phrasing.
C. John explicitly cites Moses by name in the opening sentence of his Gospel account.
D. The original Hebrew text of John's Gospel was discovered alongside the Genesis scrolls at Qumran.
ANSWER: B

Q4. How does John 1 resolve the chronological puzzle of light appearing in Genesis 1:3 days before the physical sun and moon were created?
A. It explains that Genesis 1:3 refers to atmospheric lightning caused by rapid thermal expansion.
B. It reveals that the light was Christ Himself, whose divine life was the creative light of men.
C. It shows that Moses used metaphorical poetry rather than a literal chronological sequence.
D. It clarifies that the sun was actually created in Genesis 1:1 but was hidden by heavy vapor clouds.
ANSWER: B

Q5. When Joshua encountered the man standing with a drawn sword in Joshua 5 and fell down to worship him, why was this act of worship NOT considered blasphemy?
A. Because Joshua was an anointed military commander who possessed special exemption from the Second Commandment.
B. Because the figure was a physical divine manifestation of Yahweh Himself.
C. Because Old Testament saints were permitted to worship holy angels prior to the giving of the Mosaic Law.
D. Because Joshua was actually worshipping the holy ground beneath his feet rather than the person standing before him.
ANSWER: B

Q6. According to Genesis 15:1–5 and 1 Samuel 3:10, how should we understand the phrase "the Word of the Lord came"?
A. As an audible voice.
B. As a poetic literary device used by ancient prophets to express human intuition.
C. As a physical, visible Divine Person.
D. As a secret message passed through angelic messengers to the high priest.
ANSWER: C

Q7. How does the lesson explain Jesus' statement in John 8:58, "Before Abraham was, I am"?
A. Jesus was referring to His pre-ordained selection in the mind of God before creation.
B. Jesus was asserting His pre-existence, having personally interacted with Abraham in Genesis.
C. Jesus was referring to the immortal nature that all human souls possess prior to biological birth.
D. Jesus was claiming that His earthly human lineage could be traced back genealogically before Abraham.
ANSWER: B

Q8. According to the lesson, what is the primary doctrinal difference between Christ's Old Testament manifestations and His New Testament Incarnation?
A. In the Old Testament He appeared as an angel, whereas in the New Testament He appeared as a prophet.
B. In the Old Testament He assumed temporary physical forms, but in the Incarnation He was permanently born as a true man.
C. In the Old Testament He possessed limited divine power, but in the New Testament He received full omnipotence.
D. In the Old Testament He spoke only to Israel, but in the Incarnation He spoke exclusively to the Gentiles.
ANSWER: B

Q9. What makes Christ the ultimate standard when we are commanded to be "imitators of Christ"?
A. He bypassed real human emotional limitations through His divine omniscience.
B. He lived out God's character in human shoes, proving that divine righteousness is applicable to human existence.
C. He laid down His divine attributes permanently and functioned purely as a human prophet.
D. He established an easier way suited for fragile human nature compared to the Old Testament standard.
ANSWER: B

Q10. How does the revelation of God in Jesus Christ refine our understanding of God's nature under the Law?
A. It shows that the Law were not the full expression of God's nature.
B. It proves God was vengeful in the Old Testament but changed His personality in the New Testament.
C. It shows that the Law was authored by angels rather than God.
D. It proves that God's grace did not exist prior to the birth of Jesus in Bethlehem.
ANSWER: A

Q11. How do we resolve the contradiction between Jesus "growing in wisdom" (Luke 2:52) and Jesus being the All-Knowing God?
A. Jesus only feigned ignorance in public so His peers wouldn't feel uncomfortable.
B. He is omniscient, but in His human nature He voluntarily experienced human development.
C. His divine nature completely replaced His human mind once He turned thirty.
D. Luke 2:52 is talking about spiritual growth rather than actual cognitive development.
ANSWER: B

Q12. Why is it crucial for our redemption that Jesus suffered as a genuine human rather than a divine manifestation?
A. Because if He were only divine, the crucifixion would be acting rather than actual human suffering absorbing the penalty of sin.
B. Because angels are incapable of entering physical blood covenants.
C. Because divine beings cannot experience physical fatigue or emotional sadness.
D. Because the Mosaic Law required a human priest to offer a human sacrifice to satisfy the altar's requirements.
ANSWER: A

Q13. How does the reality of hematidrosis (sweating drops of blood) substantiate the doctrine of Christ’s humanity?
A. It proves that Jesus contracted a physical disease right before His arrest.
B. It serves as proof of extreme human stress, proving He was never insulated from human pain.
C. It proves that the blood shed in Gethsemane had more redemptive power than the blood shed on the cross.
D. It demonstrates that Jesus was physically weaker than most religious martyrs.
ANSWER: B

Q14. How does the lady turned "detective" illustrate the believer's duty to "love the Lord with all your mind" (Matthew 22:37)?

C. A loving heart must constantly cross-examine church leaders to verify whether their personal life matches their doctrine
