DECK: Discipleship Experience (Section 8) Bible Experience II

Q1. What is Matthew primarily demonstrating through his repeated references to Old Testament prophecy?
A. That Jesus' ministry followed the traditions of Israel
B. That Jesus is the Messiah the Old Testament had prophesied about
C. That Jesus was a teacher who restored Old Testament practices
D. That the events of Jesus' life resembled those of earlier prophets
ANSWER: B

Q2. How does the lesson reconcile God's sovereignty with human free will?
A. Human freedom exists only when God is not involved
B. God can accomplish His purposes without overriding human choices
C. Human choices ultimately determine whether God's plans succeed
D. God's sovereignty means human choices have no real significance
ANSWER: B

Q3. What is the danger of reading Scripture without a Christ-centered lens?
A. We can miss how different parts of Scripture point to Christ
B. We may focus too much on the historical setting of the Bible
C. We may give Old Testament passages more authority than New Testament passages
D. We may interpret every passage literally instead of symbolically
ANSWER: A

Q4. Which resource is particularly useful when the difficulty lies in the meaning or usage of biblical words?
A. A devotional book
B. A biography of a biblical character
C. A lexicon or Bible dictionary
D. A collection of modern Christian testimonies
ANSWER: C

Q5. Why does the lesson describe the Bible as a "library"?
A. It contains books written for different audiences only
B. It is composed of multiple books rather than being a single literary work
C. It was originally stored in a library
D. Its books were compiled by one group of editors
ANSWER: B

Q6. What does the repetition of sacrifices communicate?
A. That the sacrifices had to be performed correctly each time
B. That Israel had not yet fully understood the purpose of the law
C. That the sacrifices themselves were not the ultimate and complete sacrifice
D. That forgiveness depended on continual participation in the sacrificial system
ANSWER: C

Q7. What assumption about divine guidance does the lesson challenge?
A. That God can use circumstances to lead people
B. That God's guidance must always come through recognizable means.
C. That Scripture is important for understanding God's will
D. That God can work through human decisions
ANSWER: B

Q8. What does biblical inerrancy mean within the lesson's framework?
A. Every biblical statement must satisfy modern scientific expectations
B. Scripture contains no errors of truth in what it teaches
C. Every biblical description must use technically precise modern language
D. Every passage must be interpreted literally
ANSWER: B

Q9. What connection does the lesson make between Joshua and Jesus?
A. Both were prophets who performed similar miracles
B. Joshua was presented as the first human king pointing toward Jesus
C. Their names are essentially the same, providing a picture pointing toward Jesus
D. Jesus repeated Joshua's military victories in His earthly ministry
ANSWER: C

Q10. Why can the Old Testament tell Christ's story without mentioning His name throughout?
A. Because the Old Testament was written mainly as historical literature
B. Because Christ is revealed through different events and prophecies that come together
C. Because the identity of the Messiah was intentionally hidden until the New Testament
D. Because the Old Testament focuses more on Israel than on individual people
ANSWER: B

Q11. What is the key distinction in the statement that God did not make Pharaoh wicked?
A. God can use the consequences of human wickedness without being its author
B. God cannot influence events involving wicked people
C. Pharaoh's wickedness was necessary for God's plan
D. Human wickedness is outside God's sovereignty
ANSWER: A

Q12. What does it mean to say that the Bible is "written for you, but not written to you"?
A. Its teachings are relevant only to its original audience
B. Modern Christians should avoid applying historical passages
C. Its message reaches us, while its original context belongs to another audience
D. Every passage requires a completely different interpretation today
ANSWER: C

Q13. What meaning of gune is highlighted in the lesson?
A. It refers exclusively to an unmarried woman
B. It can refer either to a wife or to a woman generally
C. It refers exclusively to a female church leader
D. It always refers to women collectively rather than individually
ANSWER: B

Q14. What distinguishes mature appreciation of God from a merely pragmatic relationship with Him?
A. Wanting God's blessings while remaining committed to doctrine
B. Seeking God's guidance before making decisions
C. Appreciating who God is, not merely what He can provide
D. Studying Scripture primarily to solve personal problems
ANSWER: C

Q15. When a biblical practice does not translate directly into a modern culture, what should the believer first seek?
A. The principle the practice was communicating
B. A modern equivalent with exactly the same form
C. A reason to remove the passage from application
D. The interpretation most accepted within the believer's culture
ANSWER: A

Q16. Why does the lesson reject the argument that an apparent scientific inconsistency automatically disproves Scripture?
A. Scientific knowledge is never reliable
B. Biblical writers were scientifically trained
C. Scripture should not be judged as though its purpose were to function as a science textbook
D. Scientific questions cannot be discussed from Scripture
ANSWER: C

Q17. What is the main interpretive danger of treating every biblical statement literally?
A. It can cause figures of speech to be mistaken for factual descriptions
B. It can make historical passages appear less important
C. It can cause readers to reject difficult doctrines
D. It can prevent readers from studying original languages
ANSWER: A

Q18. Who first encountered the risen Jesus?
A. Peter and John
B. The male disciples
C. The women
D. The religious leaders
ANSWER: C

Q19. What two aspects of Christ's work are emphasized in the lesson?
A. His teaching ministry and His miracles
B. His earthly ministry and His resurrection appearances
C. His public sacrifice and His heavenly intercession
D. His birth and His ascension
ANSWER: D

Q20. How does the lesson describe the Old Testament's presentation of Christ?
A. Christ is revealed gradually, in pieces and through different parts of Scripture
B. Christ is revealed almost entirely through direct Messianic statements
C. Christ is revealed mainly through the writings of the prophets
D. Christ is revealed only through the sacrificial system
ANSWER: A

Q21. How does Romans 16 describe Phoebe?
A. As a prophet and teacher
B. As a servant/deacon
C. As an apostle and elder
D. As a missionary who planted churches
ANSWER: B

Q22. When a biblical passage is difficult, what should guide its interpretation?
A. Clearer and better-established biblical evidence
B. The interpretation most commonly accepted by Christians
C. The meaning that appears most literal at first reading
D. The cultural assumptions of the modern reader
ANSWER: A

Q23. The "jigsaw puzzle" picture of Scripture mainly emphasizes what?
A. Every biblical passage can be understood independently
B. The Bible contains unrelated pieces that require different methods of interpretation
C. Individual pieces of revelation come together to give a fuller picture of Christ
D. Difficult passages should be ignored until all other passages have been understood
ANSWER: C

Q24. What concept does the lesson identify as a primary way God can direct events?
A. Divine providence
B. Human intuition
C. Audible instruction
D. Spiritual symbolism
ANSWER: A

Q25. According to the lesson, what resource cannot simply be replaced by independent study tools?
A. A newer Bible translation
B. A Bible teacher
C. A Bible dictionary
D. A commentary
ANSWER: B

Q26. Joseph's brothers unintentionally contributed to the very outcome they were trying to prevent. What does this illustrate?
A. Human opposition can unknowingly become part of providential fulfillment
B. God removes human responsibility when He has a plan
C. Joseph's brothers were unknowingly righteous
D. Divine plans require people to understand them beforehand
ANSWER: A

Q27. According to the lesson, Galatians 3:28 primarily reinforces what?
A. Men and women should have identical responsibilities in every setting
B. There is no distinction between men and women in their standing in Christ
C. Women were given authority over men within the church
D. Cultural distinctions between men and women no longer existed
ANSWER: B

Q28. How does the lesson approach 1 Corinthians 14:34, where women are told to keep silent?
A. It interprets the instruction within the chapter's context of spiritual gifts, prophecy, order, and confusion
B. It treats the instruction as a universal command against women speaking in church
C. It concludes that Paul changed his position on women between 1 Corinthians and Romans
D. It interprets "silence" as a command for women to avoid all forms of public ministry
ANSWER: A

Q29. What does the lesson's example of Cornelius and Peter primarily emphasize?
A. God can use people as instruments in communicating foundational truth
B. Spiritual experiences are unnecessary for understanding Scripture
C. Every believer must receive revelation directly from God
D. Lens through which they see the world
ANSWER: A
